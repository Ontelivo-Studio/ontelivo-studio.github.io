Privacy policy

Plain version: the game has no accounts, keeps your progress on your phone, and collects usage statistics linked to a random ID, not to your name. You can switch them off. This page explains the details.

Who we are

The controller of your data for Chess Abstract and this website is Goran Košutić, an individual based in Serbia who publishes games under the name Ontelivo ("Ontelivo", "we"). You can reach us at support@ontelivo.com.

We follow the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the UK GDPR and the Serbian Law on Personal Data Protection. The legal bases below are named after the GDPR; the UK and Serbian laws have the same ones.

Stored on your phone

Chess Abstract keeps your progress, coins, settings and chosen language on your device. We never receive this information. It is deleted when you uninstall the game or clear its data. If Android Backup is turned on for your phone, Android may keep a copy of this progress in your own Google account so it can be restored on a new phone; we cannot see that copy. The game also keeps track on your phone of how your levels go and uses it to choose how hard the next level is. The game does not ask you to create an account and does not ask for your name or email address.

The same numbers about how your levels go that the game uses on your phone to choose difficulty are sent to Firebase Analytics, so we can understand how hard the game is. They are not used to personalise ads or to build profiles for other purposes.

Analytics and crash reports are optional. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland the game shows Google's consent message when you start it. Until you agree, analytics events are sent without identifiers and no crash reports are sent. Elsewhere, collection starts right away. In every region you can switch Analytics off in the game's Settings, which also stops crash reports, and where the consent message applies you can change your answer under Privacy options.

We do not collect your name, email address, contacts, photos or precise location. If you share your daily result using the Share button, the text goes through the sharing app you pick, not through us.

Ads

The game is free and can show rewarded ads from Google AdMob: short ads you choose to watch in exchange for an in-game reward. We do not use any other ad network. To choose, show and measure ads, Google uses your phone's advertising ID, device information, approximate location from your IP address and how you interact with the ad. For this, Google decides itself how the data is used and is responsible for it as an independent controller under Google's privacy policy; we do not receive this data.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland the game asks for your choice in Google's consent message before any ad is requested, and lists only Google as the ad partner. If you do not agree, you get no personalised ads and you can play normally. You can change your answer at any time in the game's Settings under Privacy options. On Android you can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's Settings under Privacy, Ads.

Purchases

If you buy coins or other items, the payment is handled entirely by Google Play. We never see your card or bank details. We receive confirmation that a purchase was made so we can give you the item.

Who receives your data

We do not sell your data. Google processes it for us, under Google's data processing terms, in Firebase (Analytics, Crashlytics, Remote Config), Google Analytics, Google BigQuery and Google Play. For ads, Google AdMob is an independent controller, as explained under Ads.

Google may process data outside your country, including in the United States. For data from the EU, the UK and Switzerland it relies on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and its UK and Swiss extensions, or on standard contractual clauses; for other countries, including Serbia, on the safeguards in its data processing terms. You can ask us for details of these safeguards. Google's own privacy policy: policies.google.com/privacy.

How long we keep it

Analytics data is kept in Google Analytics only for the retention period we set there, currently 14 months for individual events and identifiers, after which it is deleted automatically. We also copy the analytics events into Google BigQuery, a Google database we use to study how the game is played; these copies are kept for at most 26 months from the day of the event, and earlier deleted when we no longer need them or when you ask us to (see your rights below). Crash reports are kept by Firebase Crashlytics for 90 days, after which Google deletes them. Anonymised or aggregated statistics that cannot be tied to a device may be kept without a time limit. We may also keep data longer where the law requires it, or where we need it to handle a legal claim or enforce our terms; if data cannot be removed straight away, for example from a backup, we protect it from other use until it is deleted. Emails you send us are kept as long as needed to answer you and for a reasonable time after, then deleted.

Your choices and rights

  • Stop all collection: uninstall the game. This also removes your local progress. To stop analytics and crash reports but keep playing, switch Analytics off in the game's Settings.
  • Change your consent: where Google's consent message applies, open Settings, then Privacy options in the game.
  • Reset your advertising ID or opt out of personalised ads in your Android settings.
  • Right to object: where we rely on legitimate interest, you can object at any time. The quickest way is to switch Analytics off in the game's Settings; you can also write to us.
  • Your rights: depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR, the UK GDPR or Serbian law) you may ask to see, correct, delete or export data about you, to restrict its use, and to withdraw consent. When you ask us to delete data, we delete it from Google Analytics and from our BigQuery copies. Because the game has no accounts, we usually cannot tell which records are yours unless you give us your app instance ID, which we can explain how to find. Write to support@ontelivo.com. We do not make decisions about you by automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects. We aim to answer within one month; if we need longer we will tell you why. We may ask you to confirm who you are first, and if we cannot do everything you ask, we will explain why. We may keep some data, for example payment and support records, where the law requires it.
  • Complaints: please write to us first so we can fix it. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority: in the EU, the one where you live or work; in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office; in Serbia, the Commissioner for Information of Public Importance and Personal Data Protection.

Children

Chess Abstract is meant for people aged 16 and over and is not directed at children. When you first open the game it asks you to confirm that you are 16 or older. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 16. If you believe a child has given us data, write to us and we will delete it.

This website

This website sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads no third-party scripts, fonts or trackers. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, whose operator may keep standard server logs such as IP addresses (see GitHub's privacy statement). If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you write.

Changes and contact

If we change this policy, we will update the date at the top and, for changes that matter, tell you in the game. Questions: support@ontelivo.com.
